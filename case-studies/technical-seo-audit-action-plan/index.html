Technical SEO Audit & Action Plan

Find the technical issues holding a website back and fix them in order

A technical SEO audit checks whether search engines can find, crawl, understand and index a website, and whether visitors get a fast, usable experience. The action plan then ranks the fixes.

Technical problems are often invisible to the practice, which is why a structured review is valuable.

These are independent portfolio studies based on publicly available information and third-party SEO estimates. The businesses were not contacted, are not clients and have not endorsed this work. No traffic, ranking or patient results are claimed.

Why Every Business Needs a Technical SEO Audit

Search engines must reach your pages

Crawling and indexing issues can keep good pages out of search.

Speed affects visitors

Slow pages frustrate users and weaken the experience.

Mobile matters

Most patients search from a phone.

Duplicate versions confuse

Competing URL versions send mixed signals.

Markup helps interpretation

Correct titles and structured data clarify the page.

Order matters

A ranked plan fixes the biggest problems first.

My Approach

Five stages, each with a clear purpose.

  1. Crawl and Index Review

    I check crawlability, sitemaps, canonicals and redirects.

  2. Page-Level Checks

    I review titles, headings, links and images.

  3. Experience Review

    I assess mobile usability and page performance.

  4. Structured Data Review

    I check relevant markup for accuracy.

  5. Prioritized Action Plan

    Fixes are ranked by impact and effort.

What Each Study Includes

  • Technical issue list with severity
  • Page speed and mobile findings
  • Indexing and duplicate URL review
  • Step-by-step action plan

Expected ROI

Technical fixes rarely produce instant results, but they remove obstacles that limit everything else.

Where the Return Comes From

Better crawling

Important pages are easier to find and index.

Faster pages

Visitors face fewer delays.

Cleaner signals

Fewer conflicts between page versions.

A solid base

Content and local work build on healthy foundations.

What I Would Measure

  • Indexed pages and coverage errors
  • Page speed and mobile usability results
  • Number of resolved technical issues
  • Organic impressions and clicks
  • Contact requests from the website

A Note on Results

Rankings, traffic and patient numbers cannot be guaranteed. Targets in these reports are planning goals, and the first month sets the baseline. Real return should be judged against your own data, such as the lifetime value of a new patient.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a new website?

Not necessarily. Many issues are fixed with technical updates and content improvements.

Will fixing issues raise rankings?

It can help, but rankings depend on many factors and cannot be guaranteed.

Who implements the fixes?

Depending on scope, I can implement approved changes or give clear instructions to your developer.
