Location Page SEO Strategy

Plan genuine location pages that serve real local demand

Location pages help practices show up for searches in nearby towns and neighborhoods. Done well, they give patients useful local information. Done badly, they are repetitive pages that add little.

This strategy decides which areas deserve a page, what each should contain and how pages connect to services.

These are independent portfolio studies based on publicly available information and third-party SEO estimates. The businesses were not contacted, are not clients and have not endorsed this work. No traffic, ranking or patient results are claimed.

Why Every Business Needs a Location Page Strategy

Patients search by place

Many searches include a town or neighborhood.

Thin pages do not help

Pages with only the city name changed offer little value.

Distance matters

Pages should reflect where patients actually travel from.

Pages need links

Location pages should connect to services and the profile.

Local signals build trust

Real directions, area details and team information help.

Demand should lead

Search demand and competition decide priorities.

My Approach

Five stages, each with a clear purpose.

  1. Review the Service Area

    I look at patients’ travel patterns and nearby communities.

  2. Check Demand and Competition

    I review keyword demand and who ranks.

  3. Prioritize Locations

    I tier areas by value and feasibility.

  4. Define Page Content

    Each page gets unique, useful local content.

  5. Plan Linking and Tracking

    Pages link to services and are measured.

What Each Study Includes

  • Prioritized list of locations
  • Page outlines with unique content ideas
  • Internal linking plan
  • Measurement plan

Expected ROI

Location pages pay back when they reach nearby patients with relevant, helpful content.

Where the Return Comes From

More relevant local reach

Pages match searches that include nearby areas.

Stronger local signals

Consistent information supports your profile.

Efficient effort

Pages are created only where justified.

Better user experience

Visitors find directions and services quickly.

What I Would Measure

  • Impressions and clicks to location pages
  • Local keyword rankings
  • Calls and direction requests
  • Contact requests from location pages
  • New patients by area, where tracked

A Note on Results

Rankings, traffic and patient numbers cannot be guaranteed. Targets in these reports are planning goals, and the first month sets the baseline. Real return should be judged against your own data, such as the lifetime value of a new patient.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many location pages do I need?

As many as justified by demand, distance and competition. Not one for every town.

Can I reuse the same text for each area?

No. Near-identical pages add little value and may hurt quality.

Do these pages replace my Google profile?

No. They support it. Both need consistent information.
